# Heater Supply Boundary Calibration

## Status

Accepted

## Decision

This decision supersedes the calibration and auxiliary-load clauses of [the automatic-heater boundary decision](0018-automatic-heater-voltage-current-boundary.md) and the resistance/measurement/raw-observation projection clauses of ADR 0001; the unaffected contract-ordering and snapshot lifecycle rules remain applicable.

Heater calibration records an operating boundary for the complete powered device. It does not claim an independently measured plate resistance or MCU power. A PPS contract current is a current-limit setting, and a PPS voltage request is a setpoint; neither is a live measurement. Uncalibrated VIN ADC values may establish relative voltage response, but are not an absolute voltage reference. OvO and its telemetry are developer validation tools, never inputs to the product calibration algorithm.

All heater-curve and thermal-model calibration records produced by the resistance-from-contract-current algorithm are absent for product purposes. They are not migrated, projected, previewed, or restored as fallback. Unrelated network and user preferences remain intact. Independently supplied RTD/VIN ADC calibration remains a separate domain; heater calibration must preserve the ADC observations and conversion assumptions it actually used.

The product scans a single actual PPS APDO, bounded by its advertised voltage/current, 21V, 3A, and 220°C. Current stages are spaced by 100mA within 1–3A. A heater-off baseline and a low-voltage load preflight determine which starting stages can sustain the board; a nominal 1A starting point is not forced. The heater is fully conducting during electrical boundary and equilibrium observations. Voltage rises from the usable PPS floor in 100mV steps. The last voltage with reproducible tracking and the first voltage with a reproducible flat response bracket the boundary. A single low VIN reading, cable drop, contract limit, or temperature equilibrium is not proof of CC operation. An ambiguous response fails that observation instead of manufacturing a resistance value.

Electrical tracking and thermal equilibrium are separate conditions. The current stage continues with bounded boundary rescans as the plate warms. After each sub-3A equilibrium, a 3A probe starts at the just-verified voltage and increases in 100mV steps for at most 10 seconds, then returns to the next 100mA stage. Tracking observations from these probes and the final 3A stage provide temperature coverage; they are classified separately from equilibria. A temperature band no wider than 1°C maintained for at least 10 seconds establishes equilibrium; a monotonic temperature ramp through that band must not qualify. Reaching 220°C immediately disables the MOS output and ends powered sampling. Failure to reach equilibrium below the cutoff is recorded as a cutoff, not fabricated as a steady point. A bounded timeout, sensor fault, detach, unsupported source, or supply stability failure disables heating and leaves the last committed calibration intact.

Each retained observation carries requested voltage/current, the CV/flat bracket and its classification, timestamp, actual WiFi-driver started/stopped state, paired raw RTD/VIN ADC codes and pre-user-calibration mV, the assumed divider supply voltage, and ADC conversion metadata. WiFi hardware state changes invalidate the observation window; association with an access point is not the grouping key. Reprojection with a corrected divider supply changes derived temperatures without rewriting the raw observations. Calibration does not determine the ADC supply voltage or auxiliary power by itself.

Normal heating uses an applicable boundary for its WiFi hardware state and exact advertised APDO voltage/current envelope. The original advertised minimum/maximum are stored separately from the usable scan floor (at least 5.5V) and the 21V scan ceiling; the 24-byte header retains both without enlarging the EEPROM domain. Calibration and automatic requests pin that selected APDO through negotiation and confirmation, including overlapping ranges. Lookup selects the highest observed current class not exceeding the contract current before checking temperature. A lower-current equilibrium must not clamp a 3A warm-up. Lookup must be conservative in temperature and current: no warmer observation may authorize a colder operating point, and a higher-current observation may not authorize a lower-current contract. Unobserved regions use the explicitly labelled preset model fallback; they are not extrapolated as measured calibration. A boundary contains whole-device load, so normal heating does not subtract a fixed auxiliary current or invent auxiliary watts. The retained record holds up to 21 equilibrium points and 20 transient 3A temperature bins. Each observation covers only the interval from its currently projected temperature through 1°C warmer; it cannot cap all higher temperatures. The current raw RTD projection determines eligibility. Gaps between these observed windows use the preset fallback, so a low-voltage transient observation cannot stall subsequent warm-up. Any conversion of an observed point to another voltage/current must state and test the load-model assumption; absence of that proof means retaining the observed safe operating point.

The thermal controller retains one explicitly estimated watt scale using the preset electrical model and ADC projection. Electrical safety is enforced by the supply boundary independently of that estimate. Controller saturation, voltage mapping, confirmed-contract PWM allocation, and diagnostics must use the same power reference; they must not mix an inferred total-load resistance with a plate-resistance curve. The absence of identifiable auxiliary power is represented as unknown, never zero or a fabricated measurement.

Every nonzero automatic output rechecks the latest confirmed contract and applicable boundary. An ordinary same-APDO transition may keep a still-safe confirmed contract conducting, without restarting soft start. A required safety reduction disables PWM before requesting the new contract, bypasses ordinary stepping and HOLD locks, and resumes only after a current safe confirmation and settling. At integer-milliwatt full scale, the corresponding voltage ceiling and 100% conduction are exact endpoints; downward quantization still applies below full scale.

The versioned boundary and a compacted trace of at most 48 samples share the existing 768-byte ThermalPlant EEPROM domain. Boundary observations use an 11-byte packed record; 50ms timestamps cover over seven hours. The maximum record occupies 765 bytes including the domain header. Compaction retains the ambient baseline, the complete powered temperature span selected from raw RTD bins, and passive-cooling points; it must not keep only the final high-temperature excitation. The recorder keeps a real powered or passive-cooling sample at least every ten seconds when no edge or temperature-step trigger fires, so a slow run cannot fall below the fit's minimum observation count. Supply scans may contain long stable intervals, so fitting integrates the retained elapsed-time intervals and relies on the physical-coefficient and residual gates instead of discarding every interval longer than ten seconds. Replay uses exactly the persisted trace. Partial commits cannot activate a boundary/model pair. Public run snapshots expose raw boundary rows separately from legacy resistance-curve fields, which remain empty.

## Rationale

For a freely varying resistance curve, one steady point at each temperature supplies one equation for each new resistance plus an unknown auxiliary load. It cannot uniquely separate those quantities. The PPS current-limit tolerance also prevents treating 100mA request increments as 100mA current measurements. The selected product capability is therefore an observed whole-device supply envelope, with raw data retained for replay, rather than an unsupported absolute electrical measurement.

The envelope depends on the source and wiring used during acquisition. It must carry the APDO identity and acquisition provenance; a different source envelope cannot silently inherit a calibrated claim. A live raw-VIN response below 95% of the retained ADC/request-voltage ratio latches automatic PPS output off until heating is stopped and explicitly restarted, preventing retries triggered by unloaded voltage recovery; it is a relative sag guard, not a measurement of absolute voltage or CC state. Absolute load validation uses the separately authorized developer bench and is reported independently of product calibration success.
