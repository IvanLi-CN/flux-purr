# Heater Supply Boundary Calibration

## Status

Accepted

## Decision

This decision supersedes the calibration and auxiliary-load clauses of [the automatic-heater boundary decision](0018-automatic-heater-voltage-current-boundary.md) and the resistance/measurement/raw-observation projection clauses of ADR 0001; the unaffected contract-ordering and snapshot lifecycle rules remain applicable. The product timing and scan-entry rules are further constrained by [the five-minute thermal plant calibration decision](0020-five-minute-thermal-plant-calibration.md).

Heater calibration records an operating boundary for the complete powered device. It does not claim an independently measured plate resistance or MCU power. A PPS contract current is a current-limit setting, and a PPS voltage request is a setpoint; neither is a live measurement. Uncalibrated VIN ADC values may establish relative voltage response, but are not an absolute voltage reference. OvO and its telemetry are developer validation tools, never inputs to the product calibration algorithm.

All heater-curve and thermal-model calibration records produced by the resistance-from-contract-current algorithm are absent for product purposes. They are not migrated, projected, previewed, or restored as fallback. Unrelated network and user preferences remain intact. Independently supplied RTD/VIN ADC calibration remains a separate domain; heater calibration must preserve the ADC observations and conversion assumptions it actually used.

The product scans a single actual PPS APDO, bounded by its advertised voltage/current, 21V, 3A, and 220°C. When the source also advertises a high-voltage Fixed PDO, its power envelope is preserved separately from APDO identity and caps calibration voltage at `P_fixed × I_scan`; this prevents a 60W/3A source from being driven to an invalid 21V/3A (63W) request. A heater-off baseline and an unloaded 3A preflight at the highest power-safe voltage establish the source contract without subjecting the board to low-voltage high-current startup. The preflight VIN must remain above the usable floor scaled from the unloaded high-voltage sample; a source collapse fails closed. The product path then requests exactly 3A, starts at the usable PPS floor, advances in 100mV steps, and records confirmed full-conduction observations after a 500ms electrical observation interval. It stops at the first reproducible source boundary or safe source ceiling, retains the last safe 3A tracking voltage, and continues at that voltage until powered temperature coverage reaches at least 100°C. Only 3A tracking points can authorize automatic heating; no request current is treated as a live current measurement. A single low VIN reading, cable drop, contract limit, or temperature equilibrium is not proof of CC operation. An ambiguous response is handled conservatively and cannot manufacture a resistance value.

Electrical tracking and thermal transient fitting are separate conditions. The product rapid path does not wait for lower-current equilibrium stages: it records the 3A electrical boundary and the powered temperature rise, then disables the heater and records the existing passive-cooling trace to 80°C when the five-minute budget permits. Reaching 220°C immediately disables the MOS output and ends powered sampling. A complete model still requires at least 100°C of powered coverage, an ambient sample, a valid passive-cooling segment, and the existing physical-coefficient and residual gates. The detailed 2.0A–3.0A equilibrium scanner remains host regression coverage only. Sensor fault, detach, unsupported source, capability loss, or supply stability failure still disables heating and leaves the last committed calibration intact; every failure and the five-minute timeout fails closed without committing a partial model.

Each retained observation carries requested voltage/current, the CV/flat bracket and its classification, timestamp, actual WiFi-driver started/stopped state, paired raw RTD/VIN ADC codes and pre-user-calibration mV, the assumed divider supply voltage, and ADC conversion metadata. WiFi hardware state changes invalidate the observation window; association with an access point is not the grouping key. Reprojection with a corrected divider supply changes derived temperatures without rewriting the raw observations. Calibration does not determine the ADC supply voltage or auxiliary power by itself.

Normal heating uses an applicable boundary for its WiFi hardware state and exact advertised APDO voltage/current envelope. The original advertised minimum/maximum are stored separately from the usable scan floor (at least 5.5V) and the 21V scan ceiling; the 24-byte header retains both without enlarging the EEPROM domain. Calibration and automatic requests pin that selected APDO through negotiation and confirmation, including overlapping ranges. Lookup selects the highest observed current class not exceeding the contract current before checking temperature. A 3A tracking-only point may authorize `safe_request_mv` for the closest colder temperature through a bounded 18°C carry window; the compact recorder stores one point per nominal 10°C bucket, while rounded raw-ADC projection can make the real adjacent-bucket gap wider. When the confirmed request exactly reaches an eligible tracking point, lookup may promote one next warmer observed 3A point inside the same bounded window; this is a measured staircase step, not a resistance conversion or an extrapolation to another current. A lower-current equilibrium must not clamp a 3A warm-up. If the boundary is valid but the current temperature is colder than its first projected observation, automatic heating must fail closed at the coldest retained 3A tracking voltage until measured coverage begins; it must not fall back to an unconstrained theoretical voltage in that cold-start interval. If no qualified 3A observation applies after that cold-start rule and within the bounded gap, normal heating uses the explicitly labelled preset model fallback. The adjacent-ADC exception remains limited to two rounded codes and a projected lead of no more than 1°C; a farther warmer point is eligible only through the confirmed-point promotion rule, and the live raw code remains required for the quantization exception. A higher-current observation may not authorize a lower-current contract. Unobserved regions use the explicitly labelled preset model fallback; they are not extrapolated as measured calibration. A boundary contains whole-device load, so normal heating does not subtract a fixed auxiliary current or invent auxiliary watts. The retained record holds up to 21 equilibrium points and 20 transient 3A temperature bins. Each transient point is eligible only through the bounded 18°C window from its projected temperature; the current raw RTD projection determines eligibility. The confirmed-point promotion prevents a compacted lookup gap from deadlocking warm-up while still requiring a measured next point and the VIN response guard. Any conversion of an observed point to another voltage/current must state and test the load-model assumption; absence of that proof means retaining the observed safe operating point. Boundaries written with the prior lookup semantics are obsolete and are rejected by the versioned decoder rather than reused.

The thermal controller retains one explicitly estimated watt scale using the preset electrical model and ADC projection. Electrical safety is enforced by the supply boundary independently of that estimate. Controller saturation, voltage mapping, confirmed-contract PWM allocation, and diagnostics must use the same power reference; they must not mix an inferred total-load resistance with a plate-resistance curve. The absence of identifiable auxiliary power is represented as unknown, never zero or a fabricated measurement.

Every nonzero automatic output rechecks the latest confirmed contract and applicable boundary. An ordinary same-APDO transition may keep a still-safe confirmed contract conducting, without restarting soft start. A required safety reduction disables PWM before requesting the new contract, bypasses ordinary stepping and HOLD locks, and resumes only after a current safe confirmation and settling. At integer-milliwatt full scale, the corresponding voltage ceiling and 100% conduction are exact endpoints; downward quantization still applies below full scale.

The versioned boundary and a compacted trace of at most 48 samples share the existing 768-byte ThermalPlant EEPROM domain. Boundary observations use an 11-byte packed record; 50ms timestamps cover over seven hours. The maximum record occupies 765 bytes including the domain header. Compaction retains the ambient baseline, the complete powered temperature span selected from raw RTD bins, and passive-cooling points; it must not keep only the final high-temperature excitation. The recorder keeps a real powered or passive-cooling sample at least every ten seconds when no edge or temperature-step trigger fires, so a slow run cannot fall below the fit's minimum observation count. The existing one-byte voltage code reserves `255` for the explicit passive-cooling phase; real heater voltage codes remain limited to `0..224` (28V). A zero-duty gap during the PPS scan remains a heating observation and cannot start the cooling acceptance rules. Records without the cooling marker are invalid, so older transient calibration records are absent rather than migrated. Supply scans may contain long stable intervals, so fitting integrates the retained elapsed-time intervals and relies on the physical-coefficient and residual gates instead of discarding every interval longer than ten seconds. The local heating peak/valley guard applies only to a short interval with unchanged conducting voltage; a long or voltage-changing PPS stage transition remains valid thermal response. Replay uses exactly the persisted trace; when compaction retains a confirmed zero-to-conducting edge, replay attributes that interval to the new confirmed power, while a confirmed off edge retains the preceding powered interval. Partial commits cannot activate a boundary/model pair. Public run snapshots expose raw boundary rows separately from legacy resistance-curve fields, which remain empty.

## Rationale

For a freely varying resistance curve, one steady point at each temperature supplies one equation for each new resistance plus an unknown auxiliary load. It cannot uniquely separate those quantities. The PPS current-limit tolerance also prevents treating 100mA request increments as 100mA current measurements. The selected product capability is therefore an observed whole-device supply envelope, with raw data retained for replay, rather than an unsupported absolute electrical measurement.

The envelope depends on the source and wiring used during acquisition. It must carry the APDO identity and acquisition provenance; a different source envelope cannot silently inherit a calibrated claim. A live raw-VIN response below 95% of the retained ADC/request-voltage ratio latches automatic PPS output off, requests the safe PPS floor directly, and clears the latch only after the current contract is confirmed and settled; the same heating session then resumes through soft start. A missing safe candidate, sensor fault, detach, or negotiation failure remains paused or stopped under its existing fail-safe rule. The response check is a relative sag guard, not a measurement of absolute voltage or CC state. Absolute load validation uses the separately authorized developer bench and is reported independently of product calibration success.
