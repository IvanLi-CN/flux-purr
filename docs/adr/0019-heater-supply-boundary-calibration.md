# Heater Supply Boundary Calibration

## Status

Accepted

## Decision

This decision supersedes the calibration and auxiliary-load clauses of [the automatic-heater boundary decision](0018-automatic-heater-voltage-current-boundary.md) and the resistance/measurement/raw-observation projection clauses of ADR 0001; the unaffected contract-ordering and snapshot lifecycle rules remain applicable. The product timing and scan-entry rules are further constrained by [the five-minute thermal plant calibration decision](0020-five-minute-thermal-plant-calibration.md).

Heater calibration records an operating boundary for the complete powered device. It does not claim an independently measured plate resistance or MCU power. A PPS contract current is a current-limit setting, and a PPS voltage request is a setpoint; neither is a live measurement. Uncalibrated VIN ADC values may establish relative voltage response, but are not an absolute voltage reference. OvO and its telemetry are developer validation tools, never inputs to the product calibration algorithm.

All heater-curve and thermal-model calibration records produced by the resistance-from-contract-current algorithm are absent for product purposes. They are not migrated, projected, previewed, or restored as fallback. Unrelated network and user preferences remain intact. Independently supplied RTD/VIN ADC calibration remains a separate domain; heater calibration must preserve the ADC observations and conversion assumptions it actually used.

The product scans a single actual PPS APDO, bounded by its advertised voltage/current, 21V, 3A, and 220°C. When the source also advertises a high-voltage Fixed PDO, its power envelope is preserved separately from APDO identity and caps the 3A tracking scan at `P_fixed × I_scan`; this prevents a 60W/3A source from being driven to an invalid 21V/3A (63W) request. After the operational 3A ceiling is reached, a 60W source with a 21V APDO receives one terminal probe at `21V` and `floor(P_fixed / 21V)` encoded to the 50mA PPS step (`2.85A` for 60W). The terminal probe is valid only when two consecutive full-conduction observations also satisfy the settled relative raw-VIN and unloaded-baseline tracking guards; a protocol 100% flag with a sagging board VIN is rejected. A valid v6 boundary stores the raw terminal evidence and the encoded power-safe terminal point (`21V/2.85A` for this source), plus a separate tracking-only 3A point at the already power-safe `source_max_mv` (20V for the 60W/3A source). Automatic heating may select the terminal point only with its encoded current and an explicit `V × I <= P_fixed` check; it never authorizes 21V/3A. A heater-off baseline and an unloaded 3A preflight at the highest power-safe voltage establish the source contract without subjecting the board to low-voltage high-current startup. The preflight VIN must remain above the usable floor scaled from the unloaded high-voltage sample; a source collapse fails closed. The product path then requests exactly 3A for tracking, starts at the usable PPS floor, advances in 500mV coarse steps, and records confirmed full-conduction observations after a 500ms electrical observation interval. Tracking requires both the settled relative raw-VIN response and at least 90% of the VIN expected from the unloaded high-voltage baseline scaled to the current request; a loaded 19.5V request that only produces about 16V therefore cannot become a tracking point. After the first loaded source-boundary response, the scanner keeps 500mV coarse movement while far below the failed request and uses 100mV steps only for the final 500mV bracket. It retains the last safe 3A point and retries the hot bracket after the plate warms by 10°C; two hot failures below `source_max_mv` do not end the scan or trigger the terminal probe. The scanner continues until the operational ceiling is reached or the independent 220°C/timeout fail-safe ends the job. The v6 decoder rejects v5 and earlier boundaries; there is no compatibility or migration path. No request current is treated as a live current measurement. A single low VIN reading, cable drop, contract limit, or temperature equilibrium is not proof of CC operation. An ambiguous response is handled conservatively and cannot manufacture a resistance value.

Electrical tracking and thermal transient fitting are separate conditions. The product rapid path does not wait for lower-current equilibrium stages: it records the 3A electrical boundary, uses coarse voltage movement before and away from the boundary and repeated hot 100mV probes for the final bracket, then disables the heater and records the fan-assisted cooling trace to 80°C when the five-minute budget permits. Reaching 220°C immediately disables the MOS output and ends powered sampling. A complete model still requires at least 100°C of powered coverage, an ambient sample, a valid cooling segment, and the existing physical-coefficient and residual gates. The detailed 2.0A–3.0A equilibrium scanner remains host regression coverage only. Sensor fault, detach, unsupported source, capability loss, or supply stability failure still disables heating and leaves the last committed calibration intact; every failure and the five-minute timeout fails closed without committing a partial model.

Each retained observation carries requested voltage/current, the CV/flat bracket and its classification, timestamp, actual WiFi-driver started/stopped state, paired raw RTD/VIN ADC codes and pre-user-calibration mV, the assumed divider supply voltage, and ADC conversion metadata. WiFi hardware state changes invalidate the observation window; association with an access point is not the grouping key. Reprojection with a corrected divider supply changes derived temperatures without rewriting the raw observations. Calibration does not determine the ADC supply voltage or auxiliary power by itself.

Auto/W65 normal heating uses an applicable v6 boundary for its WiFi hardware state and exact advertised APDO voltage/current envelope. The original advertised minimum/maximum are stored separately from the usable scan floor (at least 5.5V) and the 21V scan ceiling; the 24-byte header retains both without enlarging the EEPROM domain. Calibration and Auto/W65 requests pin that selected APDO through negotiation and confirmation, including overlapping ranges. Lookup selects the highest observed current class not exceeding the contract current before checking temperature. A 3A tracking-only point may authorize `safe_request_mv` for the closest colder temperature through a bounded 18°C carry window. When the projected temperature reaches the terminal observation, or is within the separate bounded 12°C pre-terminal handoff window, Auto/W65 may switch to the persisted terminal voltage/current pair (for this source, `21V/2.85A`) only after the same boundary and power-envelope checks; it must never synthesize 21V/3A. Explicit W100 is a separate 5A production path: it requires advertised and confirmed 5A capability, but this v6 format contains no 5A whole-device boundary calibration and W100 must not claim this 3A boundary protection. Before the terminal raw-ADC edge, the lower operational request may promote the terminal pair only when the projected temperature is 1–12°C below the persisted terminal observation; at the exact projected temperature or outside that window, the live raw-ADC code must reach the persisted observation first. Once the first 500mV terminal request is issued, the terminal point remains eligible while that one-step request is confirmed, so a one-code RTD quantization dip cannot cancel the handoff and pulse the heater. Once the terminal pair is confirmed, the bounded 3°C reverse handoff window may retain it across a small quantization dip. The terminal handoff window is the only warmer-point exception because the encoded pair is already power-safe; ordinary measured points still require their live temperature coverage. The compact recorder stores one point per nominal 10°C bucket, while rounded raw-ADC projection can make the real adjacent-bucket gap wider. A warmer observed point is never promoted before its projected temperature is actually covered, except for that power-safe terminal handoff: the current request may remain at the closest colder safe point, and a request above that point is capped to the closest colder safe point so the controller can blank, renegotiate, and resume without falling back to an unconstrained preset. This is a measured staircase, not a resistance conversion or an extrapolation to another current. A lower-current equilibrium must not clamp a 3A warm-up. If the boundary is valid but the current temperature is colder than its first projected observation, Auto/W65 must fail closed at the coldest retained 3A tracking voltage until measured coverage begins; it must not fall back to an unconstrained theoretical voltage in that cold-start interval. If no qualified 3A observation applies after that cold-start rule and within the bounded gap, Auto/W65 uses the explicitly labelled preset model fallback. The adjacent-ADC exception remains limited to two rounded codes and a projected lead of no more than 1°C; a farther warmer point is not eligible until its temperature is covered, and the live raw code remains required for the quantization exception. A higher-current observation may not authorize a lower-current contract. Unobserved regions use the explicitly labelled preset model fallback; they are not extrapolated as measured calibration. A boundary contains whole-device load, so Auto/W65 does not subtract a fixed auxiliary current or invent auxiliary watts. The retained record holds up to 21 equilibrium points and 20 transient 3A temperature bins. Each transient point is eligible only through the bounded 18°C window from its projected temperature; the current raw RTD projection determines eligibility. Any conversion of an observed point to another voltage/current must state and test the load-model assumption; absence of that proof means retaining the observed safe operating point. Boundaries written with the prior lookup semantics are obsolete and are rejected by the versioned decoder rather than reused.

The thermal controller retains one explicitly estimated watt scale using the preset electrical model and ADC projection. For Auto/W65 normal automatic heating, the available power reference is the lesser of the preset plate estimate `V²/R` and the confirmed whole-device contract input envelope `V × I_contract`; this keeps the controller's watt demand aligned with the source capability used by the boundary and transient fit. During Warmup, a nonzero Auto/W65 request targets the applicable observed `safe_request_mv` directly and, after the contract is confirmed, holds static full conduction at 100%; PPS warmup/recovery soft-start must not be expressed as PWM. The watt-to-voltage path applies to the low-power Approach/Hold phases, where an intermediate gate duty may be used only for power allocation. Electrical safety is enforced by the 3A supply boundary independently of that estimate. W100 uses its independent 5A production controller and contract qualification, without a 5A whole-device boundary claim in this format. Controller saturation, voltage mapping, gate allocation, and diagnostics must use the same power reference; they must not mix an inferred total-load resistance with a plate-resistance curve. The absence of identifiable auxiliary power is represented as unknown, never zero or a fabricated measurement.

Every nonzero Auto/W65 output rechecks the latest confirmed contract and applicable 3A boundary. An ordinary same-APDO transition may keep a still-safe confirmed contract conducting, without restarting a PWM soft start; PPS Warmup/full-speed remains either 0% during blanking or static 100% after confirmation. A required safety reduction disables the gate before requesting the new contract, bypasses ordinary stepping and HOLD locks, and resumes only after a current safe confirmation and settling. The VIN response guard treats a request as safe when it either remains within 95% of the retained calibration ratio or remains above 90% of the VIN ADC value expected for the current request; the latter allows a loaded 3A source to sit at its current-limit knee without mistaking normal cable/converter drop for source collapse. W100 instead rechecks its independent 5A confirmed-contract qualification and does not inherit the 3A boundary claim. At integer-milliwatt full scale, the corresponding voltage ceiling and 100% conduction are exact endpoints; downward quantization still applies below full scale.

The versioned boundary and a compacted trace of at most 48 samples share the existing 768-byte ThermalPlant EEPROM domain. Boundary observations use an 11-byte packed record; 50ms timestamps cover over seven hours. The maximum record occupies 765 bytes including the domain header. Compaction retains the ambient baseline, the complete powered temperature span selected from raw RTD bins, and cooling points; it must not keep only the final high-temperature excitation. The recorder keeps a real powered or cooling sample at least every ten seconds when no edge or temperature-step trigger fires, so a slow run cannot fall below the fit's minimum observation count. The existing one-byte voltage code reserves `254` for fan-assisted cooling and `255` for explicit passive cooling; real heater voltage codes remain limited to `0..224` (28V). A zero-duty gap during the PPS scan remains a heating observation and cannot start the cooling acceptance rules. Records without a cooling marker are invalid, so older transient calibration records are absent rather than migrated. Supply scans may contain long stable intervals, so fitting integrates the retained elapsed-time intervals and relies on the physical-coefficient and residual gates instead of discarding every interval longer than ten seconds. The local heating peak/valley guard applies only to a short interval with unchanged conducting voltage; a long or voltage-changing PPS stage transition remains valid thermal response. Replay uses exactly the persisted trace; when compaction retains a confirmed zero-to-conducting edge, replay attributes that interval to the new confirmed power, while a confirmed off edge retains the preceding powered interval. Partial commits cannot activate a boundary/model pair. Public run snapshots expose raw boundary rows separately from legacy resistance-curve fields, which remain empty.

## Rationale

For a freely varying resistance curve, one steady point at each temperature supplies one equation for each new resistance plus an unknown auxiliary load. It cannot uniquely separate those quantities. The PPS current-limit tolerance also prevents treating 100mA request increments as 100mA current measurements. The selected product capability is therefore an observed whole-device supply envelope, with raw data retained for replay, rather than an unsupported absolute electrical measurement.

The envelope depends on the source and wiring used during acquisition. It must carry the APDO identity and acquisition provenance; a different source envelope cannot silently inherit a calibrated claim. A live raw-VIN response below 95% of the retained ADC/request-voltage ratio latches automatic PPS output off, requests the current measured safe ceiling directly, and clears the latch only after the current contract is confirmed and settled; the same heating session then resumes through soft start. A missing safe candidate, sensor fault, detach, or negotiation failure remains paused or stopped under its existing fail-safe rule. The response check is a relative sag guard, not a measurement of absolute voltage or CC state. Absolute load validation uses the separately authorized developer bench and is reported independently of product calibration success.
